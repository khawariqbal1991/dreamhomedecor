18 Christmas Bedroom DIY Decor Ideas Anyone Can Make

Store-bought decor is fast, but a handmade piece is the one people always ask about, and most of the projects that actually get noticed take under an hour with supplies you probably already have. DIY Christmas decor also solves the biggest complaint about seasonal decorating — the cost — since paper, twine, scrap fabric, and jars you’re about to recycle anyway can outshine plenty of store options.

These eighteen projects range from five-minute no-sew ideas to slightly more involved painted pieces, and none of them require special tools or prior craft experience. Pick a few based on what materials you already have around the house, and treat the rest as inspiration for whenever you want to add something new.

1. Paper Snowflakes Taped Across the Wall

Folded paper snowflakes, cut in varying sizes from plain white or metallic paper, cost almost nothing and cover a surprising amount of wall space when scattered in a loose cluster. Use small loops of removable tape on the back of each one so they come down without any residue in January. Mix three or four different sizes for the most realistic, falling-snow effect.

2. A No-Sew Fleece Throw Blanket

Two layers of fleece fabric, cut to the same size and knotted together at the edges in fringe, make a no-sew throw blanket in under an hour with no tools beyond scissors. Choose one plain color and one plaid or print fleece for a layered look, and knot the fringe pieces snugly so they don’t loosen with use. This is one of the most beginner-friendly projects on the list and doubles as a genuinely useful blanket after the holidays.

3. Twine-Wrapped Mason Jar Lanterns

Wrapping plain twine around mason jars and dropping in a battery tea light turns basic jars into rustic lanterns for a nightstand or windowsill. Add a few sprigs of faux greenery tucked under the twine for extra texture, and use a hot glue dot every few wraps to keep the twine from slipping over time. Battery candles make this safe to leave lit overnight near bedding.

4. A Hand-Stamped Fabric Banner

Stamping or hand-painting a simple word like “Joy” or “Peace” onto a strip of plain canvas or muslin makes a personalized banner without any sewing machine required. Attach the fabric to a thin dowel with fabric glue or safety pins, then hang it with twine for an easy no-hardware install. Letting the letters be slightly imperfect actually adds to the handmade charm rather than detracting from it.

5. Painted Pinecone Ornaments

Pinecones collected outside and lightly dipped or brush-painted in white, gold, or silver at the tips make simple, natural-feeling ornaments for a tree, garland, or a bowl display. Let each pinecone dry fully upright on wax paper before handling, and add a small wire loop at the base if you want to hang them rather than pile them in a bowl. This project costs almost nothing beyond a small bottle of craft paint.

6. A Ribbon-Wrapped Embroidery Hoop Wreath

Wrapping a plain wooden embroidery hoop tightly in ribbon, then hot-gluing on a few small sprigs of greenery or berries, makes a mini wreath in about twenty minutes with no wreath frame needed. Hoops come in several sizes, so you can scale this up for a door or down for a small wall accent. Hang it with the hoop’s existing hardware loop or a simple ribbon tied at the top.

7. Salt Dough Ornaments With Handprints or Simple Shapes

A basic salt dough recipe — flour, salt, and water — rolled and cut into simple shapes or pressed with a handprint, then baked and painted, makes personal, keepsake-style ornaments for very little cost. Poke a hole for hanging before baking, since it’s much harder to drill afterward. This is a especially good project if kids are helping, since the dough is completely non-toxic to handle.

8. A Painted Wood Slice Garland

Thin wood slices, painted with simple shapes like trees or stars and strung together with twine, make a rustic garland to drape along a shelf or headboard. Sand the edges lightly before painting so the twine threads through cleanly, and seal with a matte spray sealant if you want the garland to last beyond one season. This project has a distinctly handmade, farmhouse feel that pairs well with natural greenery.

9. A Photo Ornament Display Using a Small Branch

A single bare branch in a vase, hung with small printed photos clipped on with mini clothespins, makes a personal, ever-changing display that works well on a dresser or nightstand. Print photos small — around two by three inches — so they hang proportionally from the branch without weighing it down. This is an easy way to add a personal, non-store-bought touch to the room’s decor.

10. A DIY Light-Up Star Made From Wood Dowels

Five wood dowels glued into a simple star shape, wrapped in warm string lights, makes an oversized light-up star for the wall without needing any woodworking experience. Glue the dowels at their crossing points first and let them fully set before adding the lights, so the shape holds without extra bracing. Hang the finished star with two small wall hooks positioned at the top points.

11. Fabric Scrap Garland for the Headboard

Torn strips of leftover fabric — old flannel shirts, scrap quilting cotton, ribbon ends — tied onto a length of twine make a soft, textured garland that costs nothing beyond what’s already in a scrap bin. Vary the fabric prints and colors within one palette so the garland feels cohesive rather than random. Drape it along the headboard or a shelf the same way you would a store-bought version.

12. Painted Glass Jar Votives With Frosted Effect

A light coat of frosted spray paint or a baking-soda-and-glue mixture brushed onto plain glass jars gives them an etched, frosted look that mimics much more expensive store-bought votives. Let each coat dry fully before adding a second for even coverage, and drop in a battery tea light once the paint has fully cured. Group three or four in different heights for the best effect on a windowsill or dresser.

13. A No-Sew Tree Skirt for a Tabletop Tree

A circle of felt, cut with a simple slit and hole to fit around a tabletop tree’s base, makes a no-sew tree skirt in a few minutes with just fabric scissors. Cut a scalloped or fringed edge for extra detail, or leave it plain for a cleaner look. This project scales down easily for the mini trees that work well in small bedrooms.

14. A Cinnamon Stick and Twine Wreath

Cinnamon sticks hot-glued around a small wreath form or cardboard ring, wrapped with a twine bow, make a fragrant, rustic wreath that fills the room with scent as it warms slightly overnight. Layer the sticks at slightly different angles rather than perfectly parallel for a fuller look. This is a good project for anyone who wants a scented alternative to candles or diffusers.

15. Hand-Painted Ornament Wall Hooks

Plain wall hooks, hand-painted in a metallic or seasonal color and mounted in a row, give you a functional spot to hang scarves, bags, or a wreath while doubling as a small piece of styled decor themselves. Two coats of paint with full drying time in between gives the most even, durable finish. This project turns an everyday functional item into something that still looks intentional once December ends.

16. A DIY Pom-Pom Garland

Yarn wrapped around a fork or a small cardboard template, then tied off and trimmed into pom-poms, strung together on twine, makes a soft, playful garland with almost no cost beyond a skein or two of yarn. Vary the pom-pom sizes and colors within one palette for the most professional-looking result. This project is easy enough for kids to help assemble, even if an adult handles the trimming.

17. A Painted Canvas Winter Scene

A simple, abstract winter scene — a few tree silhouettes, a soft gradient sky, a scattering of dot “snow” — painted on a plain canvas gives you large-scale wall art without any advanced painting skill required. Keep the composition loose and abstract rather than attempting a detailed, realistic landscape, since simplicity is what makes this approachable for any skill level. Size the canvas to at least two feet wide so it holds its own on the wall.

18. A Repurposed Jar Snow Globe

A small figurine glued to the inside of a jar lid, filled with water, a drop of glycerin, and a pinch of fine glitter, makes a simple homemade snow globe from materials most people already have. Glue the figurine and let it cure fully before sealing the lid with hot glue to prevent leaks, and shake gently to test before displaying. Line up two or three on a shelf for a charming, handmade collection.

Where I’d Start If I Only Did Three Things

If I only had an evening, I’d start with the paper snowflakes from idea one, since they cost nearly nothing and fill the most wall space for the least effort. Second, I’d make the twine-wrapped mason jar lanterns from idea three, because they add warm ambient light using jars most people are about to recycle anyway. Third, I’d try the no-sew fleece throw from idea two, since it’s the one project here that keeps being useful long after the holidays are packed away.

FAQ

Which of these DIY projects is best for a total beginner?

The paper snowflakes and the no-sew fleece throw are the easiest starting points, since both need only scissors and no gluing, painting, or drying time to get right. They’re also the most forgiving if the first attempt doesn’t come out perfectly symmetrical.

What supplies should I keep on hand to make several of these projects at once?

Twine, hot glue, plain fleece or canvas fabric, and a bag of craft paint cover the majority of projects on this list. Most of the rest — jars, branches, pinecones — are things you can gather from around the house or yard rather than buy new.

Are any of these projects safe to do with kids?

The salt dough ornaments, paper snowflakes, and pom-pom garland are all kid-friendly, using non-toxic materials and simple techniques. Save the hot glue and spray paint steps in other projects for an adult to handle, and use battery tea lights instead of real candles in anything kids will be near.

How long do handmade paper or fabric decorations actually last?

Stored flat in a bin between seasons, most paper and fabric projects on this list hold up for several years before needing to be remade. Anything with real candles, water, or organic material like pinecones or cinnamon will have a shorter lifespan, but they’re also the cheapest to remake each year.

Conclusion

A handmade Christmas bedroom doesn’t need to look homemade in the wrong way — with a little attention to color and placement, these eighteen projects hold up next to anything from a store while costing a fraction of the price. Start with whatever materials you already have lying around, pick two or three projects rather than attempting all eighteen at once, and the room will feel personally decorated in a way store-bought pieces alone never quite manage.
