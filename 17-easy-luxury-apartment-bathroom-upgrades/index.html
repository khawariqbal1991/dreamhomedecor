17 Easy Luxury Apartment Bathroom Upgrades That Look Expensive

Renting doesn’t have to mean living with a bathroom that feels temporary or unfinished — nearly every upgrade that makes a bathroom look genuinely expensive can be done without drilling, painting permanently, or losing a security deposit. Peel-and-stick materials, adhesive mounts, and simple hardware swaps deliver most of the visual impact of a full renovation while staying entirely reversible.

These seventeen upgrades are all designed with rentals specifically in mind, keeping the original fixtures and finishes safely stored for move-out. Below are a few tips worth keeping in mind as you work through the list.

1. A Removable Faucet Sprayer or Handle Upgrade

A tool-free, snap-on faucet handle or aerator upgrade in a matte black or brushed brass finish updates the sink’s most touched surface without any plumbing changes or landlord permission needed. Keep the original hardware stored safely so it can go back on before moving out. This is one of the highest-impact, fully reversible swaps available to renters.

2. Peel-and-Stick Tile Over an Existing Backsplash

Peel-and-stick tile applied directly over a dated or worn backsplash instantly updates the look of the vanity area without removing anything underneath. Clean and dry the surface thoroughly before applying for the best adhesion and easiest eventual removal. This is one of the more dramatic visual changes achievable in a single afternoon without any tools beyond scissors and a level.

3. A Tension Rod Shower Curtain Upgrade

Replacing a thin, sagging shower curtain rod with a sturdier tension rod, paired with a heavier, weighted curtain, instantly makes the shower area look more substantial and hotel-like. No drilling is required, since tension rods rely on pressure rather than mounting hardware. Choose a curved tension rod if your space allows for even more usable shower room.

4. Command-Strip Mounted Floating Shelves

Heavy-duty adhesive strips rated for several pounds can mount a slim floating shelf above the toilet or beside the vanity without a single hole in the wall. This adds real storage and styling opportunity in a rental where drilling isn’t allowed. Check the weight rating carefully and stay under it, since overloading adhesive-mounted shelves is the most common way this upgrade fails.

5. A Frosted Window Film for Instant Privacy and Polish

A static-cling frosted window film applies without adhesive and can be removed cleanly, updating a plain or outdated window while maintaining natural light and adding privacy. This looks considerably more finished than a plain pane of glass or a dated curtain. It’s one of the quickest projects on this list, typically taking under twenty minutes.

6. Removable Wallpaper on a Single Accent Wall

Peel-and-stick wallpaper applied to just one wall delivers a dramatic decorative upgrade while remaining fully landlord-friendly and reversible at move-out. Choose a pattern scale proportional to the wall size and apply it to the wall most visible from the doorway for the biggest visual return. This is one of the more involved projects on this list but still achievable in an afternoon without professional help.

7. A New Toilet Seat for an Instant Refresh

A worn or discolored toilet seat is one of the most noticeable signs of an outdated bathroom, and replacing it is a simple, tool-light project that takes less than fifteen minutes. Choose a slow-close hinge model for a more upscale, quiet feel. This is one of the cheapest items on this entire list relative to how much it improves the room’s overall polish.

8. Cabinet Contact Paper for a Vanity Refresh

Adhesive contact paper in a wood grain, marble-look, or solid color applied to a dated vanity cabinet front updates its appearance without any painting, sanding, or permanent alteration. Choose a textured or matte finish over a glossy one for the most convincing, higher-end look. This is fully removable at the end of a lease with careful, slow peeling.

9. A Standing Ladder Shelf for Extra Storage and Style

A freestanding ladder shelf, requiring no wall mounting at all, adds both storage and styling opportunity in a small rental bathroom without any installation whatsoever. Style it with folded towels, a small plant, and a candle for an immediate designer touch. This is one of the simplest additions on this list, since it involves no tools or wall damage of any kind.

10. A New Shower Head With a Universal Fit

A high-quality rain shower head or handheld combo with a standard universal threaded fitting installs by hand in minutes without any plumbing tools, dramatically improving the shower experience. Keep the original shower head stored to reinstall before move-out. This is one of the highest-impact functional upgrades available to renters, changing the daily experience of the room rather than just its appearance.

11. A Bath Mat and Towel Refresh in a Single Palette

Replacing mismatched bath mats and towels with a coordinated set in one neutral tone is one of the fastest, least expensive ways to make a rental bathroom feel considerably more put-together. Choose a plush, heavier weight for a more upscale feel underfoot and in hand. This requires zero installation and can be done in the time it takes to unpack a shopping bag.

12. A Magnetic or Adhesive Spice Rack Repurposed for Toiletries

A small adhesive-mounted rack, originally designed for kitchen spices, works well in a bathroom for organizing smaller toiletries on the inside of a cabinet door or beside the mirror, adding function without any drilling. This clever repurposing solves a real storage problem in small rental bathrooms that often lack built-in storage. Check the weight rating before loading it with heavier glass bottles.

13. A Batch of Warm-Toned LED Bulbs for Every Fixture

Swapping every bulb in the bathroom, from the vanity light to any overhead fixture, to a consistent warm color temperature is one of the simplest changes that affects how the entire room feels, without any installation risk to the fixtures themselves. This costs very little and takes just a few minutes per bulb. It’s worth doing before any other visual upgrade on this list, since lighting affects how every other change actually looks.

14. A Removable Cabinet Hardware Swap

Cabinet knobs and pulls typically attach with a single screw, making them one of the easiest hardware items to swap and restore later without any specialized tools. Choose a finish that matches your faucet or shower fixtures for a fully cohesive vanity look. Keep the original hardware in a labeled bag so reinstalling it before move-out is quick and simple.

15. A Tall Mirror Leaned Rather Than Mounted

A tall, freestanding mirror leaned against the wall, rather than a wall-mounted version, adds the visual benefit of an oversized mirror — more reflected light, a larger sense of space — without requiring any wall anchors or drilling. This works especially well in a bathroom with a bit of extra floor space near the vanity or door. It’s also fully portable if you rearrange the room or move.

16. A Small Diffuser or Candle for a Finished Sensory Layer

A reed diffuser or a quality candle in a subtle scent adds the sensory finishing touch that separates a fully styled space from one that only looks good visually. Keep the scent light in a small, enclosed bathroom. This is a purely additive change with zero risk to a rental’s original condition.

17. A Curated, Clutter-Free Countertop as the Final Touch

Clearing the vanity countertop down to just one tray, a candle, and perhaps a small plant, while routing daily products into drawers or a basket, gives even a modest rental bathroom a genuinely expensive, hotel-like polish. This costs nothing beyond a bit of reorganizing and pulls together every other upgrade on this list into one cohesive final look. It’s worth doing last, once the other changes are in place, so you can see the full room before deciding what stays visible.

Where I’d Start If I Only Did Three Things

If I only had a weekend, I’d start with a warm-toned LED bulb swap from idea thirteen, since it’s nearly free and affects how every other change in the room actually looks. Second, I’d add peel-and-stick tile over the backsplash from idea two, because it delivers one of the most dramatic visual transformations achievable in a single afternoon. Third, I’d finish with a curated, clutter-free countertop from idea seventeen, since it pulls every other upgrade together into one cohesive, genuinely expensive-looking result.

FAQ

Will any of these upgrades violate my lease or risk my security deposit?

Every idea on this list is designed to be fully reversible, using adhesive, tension, or simple screw-based hardware rather than permanent alterations like drilling or painting. Still, it’s worth checking your specific lease terms before starting, since some buildings have stricter rules than others about even removable changes.

What’s the single most impactful upgrade for the least money?

Swapping to warm-toned LED bulbs throughout the room costs very little and changes how the entire space feels, since lighting affects the visual impact of every other decor choice. A new toilet seat is a close second, since a worn one is one of the most noticeable signs of an outdated bathroom.

How do I make sure peel-and-stick materials come off cleanly when I move out?

Test the product on a small, hidden section of the wall or surface first, and always remove it slowly and at a low angle rather than pulling straight back, which reduces the risk of damaging paint or a cabinet finish underneath. Quality matters here — cheaper adhesive products are more likely to leave residue than reputable, purpose-made removable brands.

Can I actually replace a faucet or shower head in a rental, or is that only for homeowners?

Many faucet handles and shower heads use standard, universal threaded fittings that install and remove by hand without any specialized plumbing tools, making them genuinely accessible for renters. Just be sure to keep the original hardware safely stored so it can go back on exactly as it was before your final walkthrough.

Conclusion

A rental bathroom can look genuinely expensive without a single permanent change, since nearly every high-impact upgrade on this list relies on adhesive, tension, or simple reversible hardware rather than renovation. Work through the list in order of cost and impact, keep everything original safely stored, and the room will feel like a considered, upgraded space rather than a temporary one.
