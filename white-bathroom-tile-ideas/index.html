16 White Bathroom Tile Ideas That Make Your Bathroom Look Modern

White tile is the safest choice in a bathroom and, handled well, one of the most modern. The trick isn’t the tile itself — it’s the format, the layout, and the grout, which is where a plain white bathroom turns either flat and dated or crisp and current.

Each of these 16 white bathroom tile ideas is a complete look, a whole bathroom built around one tile treatment — from seamless large-format slabs to vertical subway to glossy zellige. Find the scheme that fits your space, and use it as a blueprint for a bathroom that reads clean, bright, and genuinely modern.

1. The Large-Format Slab Bathroom

This whole bathroom uses big, large-format white tiles with the thinnest possible grout lines, so the walls read as smooth, near-seamless planes. Fewer lines is what makes white tile look modern rather than dated.

Run floor to ceiling, the effect is calm and architectural. A warm oak vanity and black tap keep it from feeling clinical — the complete look is the cleanest, most current way to do an all-white bathroom.

2. The Vertical Stacked Subway Bathroom

This entire bathroom modernises the classic subway tile by stacking it vertically in a clean grid instead of the traditional brick offset. The vertical lines draw the eye up and read fresh and contemporary.

It’s the same affordable tile, laid in a way that looks designed. The complete vertical-stack look gives a small bathroom height and a crisp, modern edge for very little money.

3. The White Tile With Dark Grout Grid

This whole bathroom turns plain white tile graphic by switching to dark grout. The charcoal lines outline every tile, creating a crisp grid that reads modern and deliberate instead of builder-basic.

Dark grout also hides the discoloration that makes white grout look tired. The complete look is one of the cheapest ways to give white tile a contemporary, high-contrast edge.

4. The Glossy Zellige Bathroom

This entire bathroom uses glossy white zellige — handmade-look tiles with subtle tonal variation and an uneven glaze that catches light differently across the wall. The result is white tile with warmth and movement.

The imperfect, artisan surface reads modern and high-end at once. Paired with brass, the complete zellige look is a current favourite that gives a white bathroom soul rather than flatness.

5. The Floor-to-Ceiling White Wet Room

This whole bathroom tiles everything — floor, walls, and a doorless walk-in shower — in the same white tile for a seamless, continuous look. With no breaks between zones, the room reads larger and resolutely modern.

The single-surface approach is sleek and easy to clean. The complete wet-room look is the most architectural way to use white tile, wrapping the room in one calm, bright material.

6. The White Herringbone Bathroom

This entire bathroom adds quiet pattern by laying white tile in a herringbone weave. The diagonal interlock brings movement and a tailored, designed quality while keeping the all-white palette calm.

Herringbone reads more refined than a straight lay without adding any colour. The complete look is the choice for a white bathroom that wants subtle texture and a touch of classic-modern polish.

7. The White Tile and Warm Wood Bathroom

This whole bathroom keeps the walls crisp white but warms the room with oak — a wood vanity, wood shelving, and a wood-framed mirror. The contrast of clean tile and warm grain is the modern, Scandinavian-leaning look.

The wood stops the white from reading cold or clinical. The complete white-and-wood combination is the current answer to the sterile all-white bathroom: bright but genuinely warm.

8. The Glossy White Brick-Lay Bathroom

This entire bathroom does the classic glossy white subway in a brick offset, but pairs it with matte black fixtures to push it modern. The sheen bounces light while the black hardware adds the crisp, current contrast.

It’s the timeless tile, updated by what surrounds it. The complete look proves the most familiar white tile reads modern when the vanity, mirror, and tap are sharp and black.

9. The Textured 3D White Tile Bathroom

This whole bathroom adds depth with sculptural 3D white tile on a feature wall — raised geometric or wave shapes that catch light and throw soft shadows. It’s pattern and texture from a single white colour.

The dimensional surface reads architectural and modern. The complete look keeps the clean white palette while giving one wall genuine sculptural interest.

10. The White Penny-Round Bathroom

This entire bathroom pairs small white penny-round floor tile with larger white wall tile, mixing scales for a look that’s both vintage and fresh. The tiny round mosaics add texture and grip underfoot.

Mixing tile sizes is a modern designer move that keeps an all-white room interesting. The complete penny-round look is playful, classic, and current all at once.

11. The Minimalist Microcement-and-Tile Bathroom

This whole bathroom mixes white tile in the wet shower zone with smooth microcement on the surrounding walls. The blend keeps tile where it’s practical and a seamless surface everywhere else, for a pared-back modern look.

The two materials read deliberate together, soft and minimal. The complete look is the contemporary, spa-leaning way to use white tile without covering every wall in it.

12. The Half-Tiled White Bathroom

This entire bathroom tiles only the lower half of the walls in white, capped with a slim ledge and a soft warm paint colour above. The break adds a horizontal line and saves on tile while still protecting the splash zone.

The two-tone wall reads modern and a little classic. The complete half-tile look is a smart, budget-friendly way to use white tile where it matters and add warmth above.

13. The White Grid-Mosaic Feature Bathroom

This whole bathroom uses small white square mosaic tile on one feature wall, with fine dark grout creating a tight, graphic grid. The mosaic adds fine-scale texture and a crisp modern pattern.

Set against larger plain white tile, the mosaic wall pops without colour. The complete look is a contemporary way to add detail to a white bathroom through scale and grout alone.

14. The All-White Curbless Shower Bathroom

This entire bathroom features a curbless walk-in shower tiled in the same white as the floor, with no threshold to step over. The continuous tile makes the room read seamless, accessible, and thoroughly modern.

The flush floor and single glass panel keep the look minimal. The complete curbless-white scheme is the contemporary, barrier-free way to do a tiled shower.

15. The White Tile and Black Trim Bathroom

This whole bathroom frames white tile with slim black trim and a thin black border, drawing crisp lines around the white field. The graphic outline reads sharp, modern, and a little gallery-like.

The black edging ties to black fixtures for a pulled-together scheme. The complete white-and-black-trim look is the bold, contemporary way to give plain white tile real definition.

16. The Complete Modern White-Tile Bathroom

This is the full modern white bathroom: large-format wall tile, a warm oak floating vanity, a backlit mirror, a curbless glass shower, and crisp black fixtures. Every choice pushes the white toward clean and current.

Format, grout, wood, and black hardware all work together. The complete look shows how white tile becomes genuinely modern — it’s never just the tile, it’s everything you pair it with.

Where I’d Start if I Only Did Three Things

If I wanted white tile to look modern rather than dated, I’d start with the format and grout — go larger-format with minimal grout, or use a deliberate dark grout, because that single choice makes or breaks how current white tile reads. Next, I’d add warmth with oak: a wood vanity, shelf, or mirror frame stops white from going cold and clinical. Third, I’d sharpen the fixtures to matte black or brass for crisp contrast. The right tile format, warm wood, and sharp fixtures — that trio turns plain white tile into a modern bathroom.

FAQ

How do I keep a white tile bathroom from looking boring or dated?

It comes down to format, layout, and grout. Larger-format tile with thin grout, a vertical or herringbone lay, or a deliberate dark grout all read far more modern than small white tile with white grout in a plain brick offset. Add warm wood and sharp black or brass fixtures, and an all-white bathroom looks intentional and current rather than builder-basic.

Is white subway tile still in style?

Yes, but how you use it matters. Classic glossy subway in a brick offset can read dated on its own, so modern versions stack it vertically, switch to dark grout, or pair it with black fixtures and warm wood to bring it up to date. The tile itself is timeless; the layout, grout, and surrounding finishes are what make it feel current.

What grout colour looks best with white tile?

It depends on the look you want. A soft greige grout close to the tile keeps things seamless and calm, ideal for a minimal, large-format scheme. A dark charcoal grout creates a bold graphic grid that reads modern and hides grime. Bright white grout is the hardest to keep clean and tends to read flattest, so most modern schemes go slightly grey or dark.

Should I tile the whole bathroom or just part of it in white?

Both can look modern. Full floor-to-ceiling tiling reads seamless and architectural and is easiest to clean, especially in a wet room. Half-tiling with a warm paint colour above adds a horizontal line, saves money, and brings warmth. Choose full tiling for a sleek, all-surface look and half-tiling when you want some softness and a smaller tile budget.

Conclusion

White tile only looks dated when it’s treated as an afterthought. Choose a modern format and lay, get the grout right, warm the room with wood, and sharpen it with black or brass, and the most familiar tile in the world reads clean, bright, and thoroughly current. Pick the complete white-tile look that fits your bathroom — large-format, vertical subway, zellige, or dark-grout grid — and build the whole room around it for a modern result that never goes out of style.
