16 Christmas Bedroom Wall Decor Ideas You’ll Love

The wall above a bed is one of the most overlooked spots in holiday decorating, even though it’s usually the largest empty surface in the room and the first thing you see from the doorway. A garland on the mantel or a tree in the corner gets all the attention, but the wall behind your headboard is prime real estate that most bedrooms leave completely bare through December.

These sixteen wall decor ideas range from a single oversized piece to layered, multi-element displays, so there’s an option whether you want a five-minute update or a full focal-wall moment. Each one is sized and placed specifically for the wall above or around a bed, not a generic living room mantel.

1. A Statement Wreath Above the Headboard

A single oversized wreath centered above the headboard does more for a Christmas wall than a dozen small items scattered around it. Choose a wreath at least 24 inches wide so it holds its own above a full-size headboard, and hang it with a simple ribbon loop rather than a bulky hook that shows. Fresh or high-quality faux greenery both work — what matters most is scale, not material.

2. A Garland Draped Along a Picture Ledge or Shelf

If you already have a picture ledge or floating shelf on the wall, draping a garland along its length is one of the easiest ways to add seasonal texture without any new hardware. Let the garland spill slightly over the front edge in a few spots rather than lying perfectly flat, and tuck in a few pinecones or small ornaments for detail. This works especially well above a headboard that’s too wide for a single wreath.

3. String Lights Shaped Into a Simple Wall Pattern

Warm white string lights pinned into a simple shape — a star, a tree outline, or just soft loops — turn a blank wall into a focal point without any daytime clutter. Use removable adhesive clips rather than nails so the pattern comes down clean in January, and keep the shape loose and organic rather than perfectly geometric for a more natural look. This idea works well even in rented rooms where wall damage isn’t an option.

4. A Gallery Wall of Small Framed Christmas Prints

A cluster of small framed prints — vintage postcards, botanical holly illustrations, simple typographic quotes — creates a gallery-wall effect that feels curated rather than themed. Keep the frames in one or two finishes, like all black or all natural wood, so the collection reads as intentional even with different images inside. Three to five frames in a loose grid is usually enough; more than that starts to compete with the bed.

5. A Large Round Mirror With a Wreath Layered Over It

Layering a small wreath over the top edge of a round mirror gives you both function and seasonal decor in the same spot, and the mirror bounces extra light back into the room. Choose a wreath sized to roughly a third of the mirror’s diameter so it reads as an accent rather than covering the glass. This idea suits a bedroom that doesn’t have much wall space to spare for decor on its own.

6. Oversized Ornaments Hung as Wall Art

A few oversized decorative ornaments — the kind meant for outdoor trees — hung directly on the wall in a loose cluster make for unexpected, sculptural wall art. Vary the sizes and hang them at slightly different heights rather than in a straight line, and stick to two or three colors so the cluster feels styled. This idea is inexpensive and takes up almost no wall depth, which makes it good for a small bedroom.

7. A Fabric or Felt Banner Spelling Out a Simple Word

A soft fabric or felt banner spelling out a short word like “Joy” or “Merry” adds personality and color without needing frames or hardware beyond a single ribbon or dowel. Keep the banner to one focal wall — above the bed or on the wall opposite it — so it doesn’t compete with other decor in the room. A banner in your existing color scheme rather than a strictly red-and-green one will also make it easier to keep up longer.

8. A Vertical Garland Strand Framing Each Side of the Bed

Instead of decorating the wall above the bed, run a slim vertical garland strand down each side, framing the headboard like a pair of soft columns. This draws the eye up and adds height to the room, which is especially useful in a bedroom with a low or unremarkable headboard. Secure the top of each strand with a small hook or clip so it hangs straight rather than curving outward.

9. A Large Canvas or Print of a Winter Scene

A single large-scale canvas or framed print of a snowy landscape, winter forest, or softly abstract winter scene brings the season into the room without any literal Christmas imagery at all. This is a good option for anyone who wants the wall to still look right well past the holidays, since a winter landscape doesn’t scream “Christmas decor” the way ornaments or garland might. Size the piece to at least two-thirds the width of the headboard for real presence.

10. A Ribbon-Hung Collection of Vintage Ornaments

Hanging a handful of vintage or heirloom ornaments directly on the wall by ribbon loops, at varying lengths, turns sentimental pieces into a display instead of leaving them boxed until tree time. Group them loosely rather than in a strict row, and use ribbon in a single color to unify the different ornament styles. This idea works especially well for anyone with inherited or collected ornaments they want visible for more than a few weeks a year.

11. A Pom-Pom or Tassel Garland for a Playful, Modern Look

A pom-pom or tassel garland in soft neutrals or a couple of seasonal accent colors gives a more modern, playful alternative to traditional greenery. Drape it in a loose swag above the bed or along a shelf the same way you would a garland, and mix pom-pom sizes for visual interest. This option suits a more contemporary or kids’ bedroom better than traditional pine or holly-based decor.

12. A Star or Snowflake as a Single Oversized Focal Piece

One oversized star or snowflake — in wood, metal, or woven rattan — mounted alone on the wall makes a strong, minimal focal point that doesn’t require any additional decor around it. This is the lowest-effort idea on the list and one of the most effective, since a single large shape reads as intentional rather than sparse. Center it above the headboard at eye level from a standing position in the room, not directly against the ceiling.

13. A Plaid or Tartan Fabric Panel as a Soft Headboard Backdrop

Pinning or mounting a length of plaid or tartan fabric on the wall behind the bed creates a soft, seasonal headboard backdrop, especially useful if your actual headboard is plain or low. Stretch the fabric over a simple frame or use fabric wall tape for a clean, taut look rather than letting it hang loosely. This idea adds warmth and pattern to the largest visible wall in the room for very little cost.

14. Battery Candles or Lanterns Mounted as Wall Sconces

A pair of battery-powered candle lanterns mounted as faux sconces on either side of the bed adds warm ambient light and a seasonal silhouette without any wiring. Choose lanterns with a warm flickering bulb rather than a cool white one for the coziest effect, and mount them at the same height as your usual bedside lamps would sit. This idea doubles as functional lighting once December is over, so nothing needs to come down right away.

15. A Botanical Greenery Wall Hanging Without Any Ornaments

A single greenery wall hanging — eucalyptus, pine, or a mixed botanical bundle — tied simply with twine or ribbon gives a natural, understated seasonal touch for anyone who finds ornaments too literal. Hang it slightly off-center above the bed rather than dead center for a more relaxed, organic feel. This is one of the few ideas here that reads as seasonal in December and simply “botanical” for the rest of winter.

16. A Rotating Display Ledge for Cards and Small Ornaments

A slim floating ledge mounted on the wall gives you a spot to display holiday cards, a few small ornaments, or a strand of lights that you can rearrange all season long as new cards arrive. Keep the ledge shallow — three to four inches deep is plenty — so it doesn’t compete with nightstands below it. This is the most flexible idea on the list, since the display changes and grows through December rather than being fixed on day one.

Where I’d Start If I Only Did Three Things

If I only had an afternoon, I’d start with one oversized focal piece — the statement wreath from idea one or the single star from idea twelve — since one large, well-placed item does more than a dozen small ones. Second, I’d add warm string lights shaped simply on the wall from idea three, because the ambient glow changes the feel of the whole room at almost no cost. Third, I’d layer in a bit of texture with a garland along a shelf or draped down the sides of the bed from ideas two or eight, which fills out the wall without ever feeling cluttered.

FAQ

What’s the best way to decorate a bedroom wall for Christmas without damaging it?

Removable adhesive hooks, clips, or fabric wall tape hold garland, lights, and lightweight frames without leaving marks, and most rate for several pounds of weight, which covers everything on this list. Save nails only for anything you plan to leave up as permanent decor beyond the season.

How big should a wreath or focal piece be above a bed?

As a rule of thumb, aim for roughly two-thirds the width of your headboard. Smaller than that tends to look lost on a large wall, while going wider than the headboard itself can look oversized unless you’re deliberately going for a dramatic statement piece.

Can I combine several of these ideas on one wall, or is that too much?

Two elements paired together — like a wreath with string lights, or a garland with a mirror — usually works well, since one item after that mostly becomes background support rather than a whole other focal point. Beyond two, the wall starts to compete with the bedding and pillows for attention, so it’s best to pick a primary piece and one supporting layer.

Are string lights safe to leave on a wall overnight in a bedroom?

LED string lights run cool to the touch and are generally considered safe for overnight use, but always check that any strand touching fabric or paper decor is rated for that contact and unplug or use a timer for peace of mind. Avoid incandescent mini lights pressed directly against dry garland or paper items for extended periods.

Conclusion

The wall above your bed is one of the easiest places in the whole house to make a big seasonal impact, precisely because it usually starts out empty. Pick one focal piece sized to the space, add a layer of warm light or texture, and resist piling on more than two elements — and that blank wall becomes the first thing anyone notices when they walk into the room this Christmas.
